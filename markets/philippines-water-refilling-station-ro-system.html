HOW IT WORKS

Five steps from source water to refill

STEP 01

Source tank & pump

Water from your municipal pipe or well is stored and fed at steady pressure.

STEP 02

Filtration

Sand and carbon filters remove particles and chlorine, protecting the RO membranes.

STEP 03

RO — the core

Pressure pushes water through membranes that typically reject 97–99% of dissolved salts. Some water goes to drain — plan your floor drainage.

STEP 04

UV disinfection

A final barrier against bacteria after RO, keeping stored water safe.

STEP 05

Storage & filling

Purified water sits in a covered tank and fills washed containers. A daily TDS check tells you all is well.

CHOOSING

Choosing the right equipment

1. Start with your water source

Municipal supply and deep well are not interchangeable — the wrong filtration is the most common reason for early membrane replacement.

CompareMunicipal / LGU piped supplyDeep well
Water characterTreated, chlorinated; quality varies by areaOften higher hardness, iron or salinity; varies well by well
Filtration neededSediment filter + activated carbonStronger multimedia filtration, iron removal, softening
Check firstA simple TDS reading of your supplyA full water analysis (TDS, hardness, iron)

2. Match the RO unit to your daily containers

One 5-gallon container holds about 19 liters. Choose a system rated at about 2–2.5 times your daily sales — real output runs below rated output, and you need headroom for peak days.

Containers sold per dayPurified water neededSuggested system class
100 × 5-gallon≈ 1,900 L/day (≈ 500 GPD)1,000–1,500 GPD
200 × 5-gallon≈ 3,800 L/day (≈ 1,000 GPD)2,000–2,500 GPD
400 × 5-gallon≈ 7,600 L/day (≈ 2,000 GPD)4,000–5,000 GPD

MAINTENANCE

Maintenance costs

Plants fail through ignored maintenance far more often than faulty machines. Typical intervals:

ItemTypical intervalWhy it matters
Sediment / cartridge filters1–3 monthsClogged filters choke the RO pump
Activated carbon6–12 monthsOld carbon lets chlorine damage membranes
Softener salt / antiscalantOngoing top-upControls hardness scale
RO membranes2–3 yearsThe expensive item — filtration quality decides lifespan
UV lampReplace yearlyOutput fades before it visibly fails
Daily checksTDS in/out, pressuresCatches problems while they are cheap

Two buying tips that decide your maintenance bill: confirm who can service the plant locally before you buy it, and prefer industry-standard membranes and cartridges — any technician can source them. FAQ

Quick questions

How many GPD do I need for 200 containers a day?

About 1,000 GPD of purified water (200 × ~19 L). Applying the 2–2.5× rule, choose a 2,000–2,500 GPD class system.

How long do RO membranes last?

Typically 2–3 years with good filtration and on-time cartridge changes. High hardness, iron or sediment in feed water shortens membrane life.

Can one machine handle both municipal and deep-well water?

The RO stage can be the same class, but filtration differs (carbon-led vs iron/softening-led). Configure after a water analysis.
