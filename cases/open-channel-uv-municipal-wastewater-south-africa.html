CASE STUDY · OPEN-CHANNEL UV DISINFECTION

Open-Channel UV Disinfection for a Two-Station Municipal Wastewater Program in South Africa

Case published 21 September 2026 · Client anonymized

Project location
South Africa — municipal wastewater program, two treatment stations
Water duty
Disinfection of secondary-treated domestic effluent before discharge
Capacity
5,000 m³/d + 15,000 m³/d (20,000 m³/d combined), 24-hour operation
Technology
Open-channel (gravity) UV — 12 modules, 76 × 320 W high-output low-pressure lamps
Control
PLC with touchscreen HMI, UV intensity monitoring, water-level sensing, night low-flow auto shutdown
Delivery route
Supplied to an environmental EPC contractor; contract awarded December 2019, dispatch within 25 days of advance payment

Project background

A municipal wastewater program in South Africa needed a final disinfection stage for two domestic-sewage treatment stations of different sizes — 5,000 m³/d and 15,000 m³/d. Both stations run continuously, but flow follows a strong daily cycle: the peak arrives during the day, while night-time flow often falls below the level at which disinfection is needed at all.

The EPC contractor responsible for the stations asked KONCHE to size and supply the UV stage against the two design flows, and to keep operation as automatic as possible — the sites have a small maintenance team and no dedicated disinfection operator.

Site challenges

  • Strong day/night flow swing. Fixed 24-hour operation would waste power and burn lamp hours at night, when the flow threshold is not met.
  • Suspended solids shielding. UV performance depends on how much light reaches the microorganisms; elevated TSS in the effluent shields them and destabilizes the result.
  • Outdoor duty. The disinfection channels and electrical equipment sit in the open, exposed to sun and rain.
  • Two capacities, one program. The two stations needed a shared, modular equipment platform rather than two one-off designs with mixed spare parts.
  • Limited on-site labor. Sleeve cleaning, lamp replacement and fault-finding had to be simple and largely automatic.

The supplied systems

KONCHE configured two open-channel UV systems on the same modular platform, sized to each station's design flow:

Configuration5,000 m³/d station15,000 m³/d station
316 stainless steel UV modules57
High-output low-pressure lamps20 × 320 W (4 per module)56 × 320 W (8 per module)
High-transmission quartz sleeves2056
Electronic ballasts (PF 0.98)20 — one per lamp56 — one per lamp
Automatic sleeve wiping5 air cylinders + wiper racks + compressor7 air cylinders + wiper racks + compressor
ControlPLC central control with touchscreen HMI, UV intensity meter, water-level sensor, weatherproof ballast cabinet (powder-coated steel)
Power supply220 V / 50 Hz

The lamps are non-ozone 254 nm germicidal types with a rated service life of 12,000 hours and a germicidal output of at least 40% of lamp power; each ballast monitors its own lamp and raises an audible/visual alarm on failure, so a single lamp fault never silently disables the channel.

Engineering approach

  1. Size to flow, then build the civil works around it. The EPC confirmed the two design flows; KONCHE calculated the UV dose, lamp count and module dimensions, and the EPC prepared the channels to the confirmed dimensions before delivery.
  2. Deliver as pre-assembled, installable units. The systems arrived as complete modules with frames, cables and connectors. Site work was limited to placing the modules, connecting piping and cabling, and commissioning — KONCHE's commissioning engineers attended site.
  3. One platform across both stations. Identical module, lamp, sleeve and ballast types at both stations simplify stocking and maintenance, and capacity can be extended by adding modules.
  4. Flow-adaptive operation. The PLC and water-level sensor run the UV at full duty through the daytime peak and shut the system down automatically when night-time flow drops below the set threshold — saving power and lamp hours.
  5. Keep the water UV-ready. KONCHE specified upstream filtration to hold TSS at or below 10 mg/L and designed to a UV transmittance of at least 65%, so the installed dose reaches the target microorganisms.
  6. Clean the sleeves pneumatically. Compressed-air-driven wiper rings wipe the quartz sleeves on a programmed cycle, keeping light loss from fouling low without manual brushing.

Disinfection performance was specified against a Class-1B effluent requirement; KONCHE sized the systems to a Class-1A design margin so the stations hold compliance with room to spare as lamps age between replacement cycles.

Results in service

  • Both stations were delivered, installed and put into operation; the UV stage continues in service with KONCHE supplying follow-up lamp and sleeve replacements.
  • Disinfection operates unattended: full output through the daily peak, automatic shutdown at night low flow, per-lamp alarming and intensity monitoring through the HMI.
  • Automatic sleeve wiping removed routine manual cleaning from the site team's workload.
  • Standardized consumables keep maintenance simple — 320 W lamps (1,554 mm, single-end four-pin) and quartz sleeves (1,800 mm × Ø28 mm).

Why open-channel UV suited this program

  • No chemicals, no disinfection by-products. UV inactivates organisms in the channel without dosing or storing chemicals at the stations.
  • A natural fit for municipal hydraulics. Open channels carry the full plant flow by gravity — no added head loss through pressure vessels.
  • Modular capacity. Lamp and module counts scale with flow, as the two stations here show on the same platform.
  • Low operating burden. Flow-adaptive PLC operation, per-lamp monitoring and automatic wiping suit sites with small maintenance teams.
